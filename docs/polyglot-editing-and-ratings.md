# Original-script editing and Polyglot ratings

Romanized subdivisions are authoritative on the romanized editing surface. A cut inside the input for a single original-script character stays in the map, with its timing intact, but has no marker on the original-script surface. Opening an editor, attaching generated lyrics, and editing romanized text do not discard these cuts. Moving, adding or removing markers explicitly on the original-script surface updates the shared romanized cuts. Changing only the original spelling preserves cuts when its displayed marker pattern is unchanged.

The import-time natural syllabification pass filters its **newly generated** cuts through the available original-script character boundaries. Existing authored subdivisions are retained. This pass never runs merely because a saved map is loaded.

The editor's **Add original script** control creates empty `original` fields on words. These fields persist on save, including for a previously line-timed map. Blank fields alone do not make Polyglot available. On Japanese maps, original-script inputs convert `[ ] . ,` to `「 」 。 、` after committed input, without changing IME composition text or romanized inputs.

Retyping an already-judged character keeps the higher timing tier from its original award or its re-entry. It improves the existing score and accuracy contribution without adding another combo increment or health reward. Replays record this rule; older recordings keep their original first-award behavior. Japanese inputs improve independently when their count matches; when a different valid spelling changes the input count, the more accurate complete attempt supplies the grades.

Japanese Polyglot accepts the shown furigana as kana input for kanji, alongside romaji and direct committed kanji. Kana and romaji can be mixed in a reading. Completed kana brighten individually in the furigana; pending input can be backspaced. Grades and combo are committed when the lyric character completes: each accepted kana input contributes one grade, while combo still uses the minimum romaji input count. Japanese Polyglot autoplay uses the shortest accepted romaji spelling and a non-scoring confirmation for an ambiguous final `n`. Enter during an incomplete reading skips the remaining character and line through the normal line-skip path; confirming a complete trailing-n reading retains its confirmation behavior.

Dictionary-based Japanese generation preserves English phrases and uses curated hiragana spelling defaults for common lyric vocabulary. The [research notes and approved categories](japanese-lyric-spelling-preferences.md) distinguish exact surface/reading preferences from context-sensitive words and document the limits of automatic language detection. Authored originals and selected readings are preserved.

Map Peak, Target and Average WPM use the required input stream with Polyglot: minimum accepted romaji for Japanese, and original letters plus required composition inputs for supported Latin/Cyrillic scripts. Native-layout accented letters cost one input; ordinary Shift presses follow the nomod WPM convention. Unsupported scripts retain romanized pace figures. Changing Polyglot, Literate or the playback rate refreshes the displayed figures, including live custom-rate slider adjustments. Removed spaces between Japanese characters contribute no WPM or SR input; retained spaces still count, and timing gaps remain pauses. These map estimates differ from gameplay WPM, which counts the accepted inputs the player actually types. Both use five inputs per WPM word.

Polyglot ratings support Latin and Cyrillic originals and the existing Japanese input model. Latin/Cyrillic streams count one scoring character per original letter. Japanese uses the shortest accepted romaji input at its original-character timing targets. Unsupported original scripts keep the romanized star rating and do not receive a pp estimate in the HUD or results. Other unranked mods retain their local pp estimates.

Keyboard demand uses a fixed native-layout baseline rather than inspecting the player's OS layout. Direct native accented letters (including national AltGr letters on Polish and Romanian layouts) are exempt from composition costs. Dead-key accents require extra inputs charged at the existing Shift surcharge cost. Uppercase original letters keep the usual Literate capital/Shift behavior, including Cyrillic; ordinary case-insensitive gameplay does not charge for capitalization. The existing plain-e alternative for ë remains free of composition cost.

Polyglot is ranked for every dropdown language except Chinese and Korean, which remain unranked and local-only. The selected map's language controls mod labels, submission eligibility and ranked pp eligibility. Other unranked mods and nonstandard rates retain their existing restrictions.

The native-letter sets are centralized in `PolyglotRatingSupport.IsNativeLetter`. Layout references: [Unicode CLDR keyboard charts](https://unicode.org/cldr/charts/43/keyboards/keyboards2chars.html), [Microsoft keyboard layouts](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/globalization/windows-keyboard-layouts/). The sets distinguish direct national keys from dead-key compositions; they do not exempt every accented character that can ultimately be produced by a layout.

The language menu adds Afrikaans, Bavarian, Danish, Dutch, Norwegian, Belarusian, Bulgarian, Croatian, Czech, Serbian, Slovak, Ukrainian, Finnish, Hungarian, Catalan, Neapolitan, Portuguese and Romanian. English, Japanese, Korean, Russian, French and Spanish appear first; the remaining languages are alphabetical, followed by Instrumental and Other. Unspecified, Low German and Swiss German are omitted from the menu. Existing stored enum IDs remain unchanged. These extend the existing English, German, Swedish, Russian, Polish, Spanish, Italian and French choices. Regional-language coverage includes qualifying Germanic varieties and Neapolitan; speaker estimates and language/dialect boundaries vary.
